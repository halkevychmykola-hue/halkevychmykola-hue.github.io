Project 03 · Circuit-1 · interactive replay

Circuit-1: the 2022 replay

I traded 2022 again, week by week, without knowing the future. A cursor moved through the year, I only saw facts dated on or before it, and I made every decision myself. Afterwards an engine replayed the same trades on minute data under the rules I wrote later (protocol v2), so I could see where my results came from.

Results in R: 1R is the amount risked on one idea, set from volatility (daily volatility over 63 sessions × √T). The engine reproduces my journal to within about 0.01R per trade. The v2 rules were written after these trades, so the rule-based numbers are in-sample: they show where the money was lost, they do not prove an edge.

Risk rules

Position-size calculator

One event is one idea and one unit of risk. 1R = daily volatility × √T × entry price, the stop sits at −1R, the monthly loss limit is −1R, and at most two events can be open at once.

Pre-registered tests I rejected, 2010–2022
  • Driver variables give no out-of-sample direction (information coefficient ≈ 0).
  • The level of the supply–demand balance does not predict direction; the sign of the reaction to a report is right in 59–75% of cases.
  • After the first day, a shock is a coin flip. Sanctions and policy drift for up to 60 days. An expected shock is sold on the fact.
  • News enters the price on the day it comes out. What matters is matching the holding period to the catalyst; breakeven stops and trailing do not help.
  • Mechanical entry after the USDA WASDE report: rejected. Weekly EIA data adds about +0.03R. On CPI, payrolls and FOMC only gold passes.
  • Option overlays, a bubble detector (BSADF), straddles after a crash, holding longer, forced-seller trades (t 1.63): none passed the pre-registered rule.
  • "Fear" trades in the yen, Swiss franc and short AUD: 52 trades, −24.3R, dropped.
